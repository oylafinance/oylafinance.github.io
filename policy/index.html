Cardwell

Privacy Policy

In effect from 21 October 2026

Cardwell is an iPhone app for keeping your own record of the benefits that come with your credit cards, such as statement credits, lounge visits and hotel night certificates, and of when each of them resets. It is published by OYLA FINANCE, TOO, a limited liability partnership registered in the Republic of Kazakhstan. In this policy, "OYLA FINANCE", "we", "us" and "our" refer to that partnership; "Cardwell" and "the app" refer to the app on your iPhone together with its widgets.

The only personal information that can reach us is described under Emails you send us. For that information, OYLA FINANCE is the controller, and under the law of Kazakhstan it is both the owner and the operator of the database that holds it.

Nothing you enter is sent to us

Cardwell has no sign-in, no server of ours behind it and no code that connects to the internet. Whatever you type stays on your iPhone, and we have no means of looking at it. There is no analytics, advertising or tracking in the app, and its privacy label on the App Store reads "Data Not Collected". Cardwell is also kept apart from anything else OYLA FINANCE does: it shows no offers from the company, and since nothing recorded in it reaches us, none of it can be used for profiling, advertising or any decision about you. The one way personal information about you can reach us is an email you decide to send (see Emails you send us).

What you put into Cardwell

Every piece of information in the app comes from you:

  • Cards: the nickname you choose, a color and an emblem, the currency (USD, GBP, CAD or AUD), the annual fee, the day the card year starts, and whether the card is archived.
  • Benefits: a name and type, an amount or a number of uses, how often it resets, an optional "my value" figure, an optional note, and whether it is paused or has reminders.
  • Records: the day you used a benefit, the amount or count, an optional short note, and the periods you chose to skip.
  • Settings: your default currency, reminder time and profile, whether reminders and widgets show details, and whether App lock is on.

Cardwell does not want your card number and will not store one. If a nickname, a benefit name or a note contains a run of 13 or more digits, the app refuses to save it and asks you to remove them. There is no field for an expiry date, a security code, a PIN, a password, a bank login or a statement.

Where that information is kept

Everything stays on the iPhone, in these places:

  1. Cardwell's database, inside the app's private storage, which iOS keeps apart from other apps. Your cards, benefits and records are here.
  2. The app's settings, also private to the app. Besides the settings listed above, they hold three markers: whether the first card setup has been completed, the day you last saved a copy, and whether you closed the "Reminders are off" note.
  3. A small file for the widgets. So that widgets can count down without opening the app, Cardwell writes a summary of current and upcoming benefit periods to a storage area that only Cardwell and its own widgets can open: start and end dates, what is left, card colors and, while details are on, benefit names and amounts. Card nicknames are not part of it.
  4. The reminder schedule. Once you allow notifications, Cardwell gives iOS a list of upcoming local notifications with their wording. iOS stores and delivers them on the device; no push server is involved.
  5. Files you export. Created only when you ask for them (see Copies and spreadsheets).
  6. A damaged database, set aside. If the database ever fails to open, Cardwell renames it and keeps it beside a new, empty one instead of deleting it, so you can still restore from a copy. It goes away when the app is deleted.

If iCloud Backup or backups to a computer are turned on, iOS includes Cardwell's storage in those backups, as it does for other apps. Those backups are managed by you and Apple, and we have no access to them.

Reminders, widgets and the Lock Screen

Cardwell asks iOS for permission to show notifications at one of three moments: when you finish adding a card with reminders switched on, when you switch reminders on in Settings, or when you tap "Send test reminder". If you decline, nothing is scheduled and the rest of the app works as before.

While Show details in reminders and widgets is on (it is on at first), a reminder names the benefit and the day it ends, together with what is left and the card nickname, and widgets show benefit names and amounts. With the switch off (Settings > Reminders), reminders read only "Benefits ending soon" and "Open Cardwell to see what is left.", and widgets show counts without names or amounts. Two other notes can appear: "Card year ends in N days", which adds the card nickname only while details are on, and "Reminders need a refresh" with "Open Cardwell so upcoming reminders stay scheduled." Amounts in widgets are marked as private so that iOS can hide them while the iPhone is locked, and how much of any notification appears on a locked screen also follows your preview setting in iOS. The notes are quoted here in English; when Cardwell is used in Russian, they say the same things in Russian.

The Mark used button on a reminder makes iOS unlock the iPhone before Cardwell records anything.

App lock and Face ID

App lock is off unless you turn it on (Settings > Privacy). When it is on, Cardwell asks iOS to confirm that it is you, by Face ID or by your device passcode, each time you come back to the app, and it covers its screens in the app switcher. The check is done entirely by iOS. Cardwell is told only whether it succeeded; images or measurements of your face never reach the app or us.

Copies and spreadsheets

  • Save a copy (Settings > Data) puts all your cards, benefits, records and most settings (not App lock) into one file named like cardwell-copy-2026-10-27.json. The file is readable text without encryption. Cardwell prepares it in a temporary folder and opens the iOS share sheet; you choose where it goes, for example Files, iCloud Drive, AirDrop or Mail. From then on, the copy is handled by the place you sent it to, so keep it somewhere you trust. Cardwell notes only the day of the last completed save.
  • Export usage (CSV) produces a spreadsheet of one calendar year of records (date, card nickname, benefit, kind, amount, currency, note), named like cardwell-uses-2026.csv, and goes the same way through the share sheet. The sample card is left out of it.
  • Restore from a copy reads only the file you pick in the iOS file picker. Cardwell checks the whole file first and replaces what is on the iPhone only after you confirm.

No network, no third-party code

  • Cardwell contains no networking code. The Privacy Policy and Support rows in Settings open this page and the support page in your web browser; the app hands over the address and sends nothing of its own.
  • It includes no software development kits from other companies: no analytics, advertising, crash reporting or social media code.
  • It does not read the advertising identifier and does not follow you across other companies' apps or websites, which is why it never shows the iOS request to allow tracking.
  • It does not use your location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone, calendars or health data.
  • OYLA FINANCE does not sell, rent or share personal information.

What Apple reports to us as the publisher

App Store Connect gives us aggregated figures about Cardwell, such as downloads by country, ratings and reviews. If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers (the Analytics & Improvements screen under Privacy & Security in iOS Settings), Apple may also pass us crash logs and usage statistics for Cardwell that carry no name or contact details. We read crash logs only to fix crashes. This information is collected by Apple under Apple's privacy policy, not by Cardwell, and the same iOS setting turns it off.

Emails you send us

When you write to connect@oylafinance.com, we receive your email address, any name your mail app attaches, your message and its attachments. We use them to answer you and to follow up on the same question. They are not added to a mailing list, are not used to offer you anything and are not given to anyone; the only other party involved is the company that runs our email service, which handles messages only on our instructions. We delete the thread once your question is settled, or sooner if you tell us to. Please never send card numbers (Cardwell does not need them), and attach a copy file or screenshots showing amounts only if we ask for them to solve a problem.

Under the law of Kazakhstan, we handle your email with your consent, which you give by writing to us; you can withdraw it at any time by asking us to delete the correspondence. If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, the basis is our legitimate interest in replying to people who contact us (Article 6(1)(f) of the GDPR and the UK GDPR, and the Swiss equivalent).

Erasing your information

  • One record: open the benefit, tap the period, then touch and hold the record and choose to delete it (or open it and tap Delete record).
  • One benefit: open it, use the benefit menu and tap Delete benefit; its history goes with it.
  • One card: open it, use the card menu and tap Delete card; its benefits and history go with it. Archive card hides a card without deleting anything.
  • Everything you entered: Settings > Data > Delete all data removes all cards, benefits and history. Your settings remain until you delete the app.
  • The app itself: deleting Cardwell erases its database, settings, widget file, any set-aside database and its scheduled reminders. Exported files and older device backups live outside the app; deleting Cardwell leaves them as they are, and they go only when you delete them.

We keep no copy of your app data, so apart from emails there is nothing for us to erase.

Children

Cardwell is made for adults keeping track of their own credit cards. It is not directed at children and is not part of the Kids category. It collects nothing from anyone, so it collects nothing from children either, and it has no chat, profiles or way for people to contact each other. If we find that an email came from a child, we delete it.

  • EEA, United Kingdom and Switzerland (GDPR, UK GDPR): you may ask for access to, correction or erasure of personal information, restrict or object to its use, and receive it in a portable form. You may also complain to a data protection authority; in the UK, that is the Information Commissioner's Office.
  • California and other US states with privacy laws (including the CCPA as amended by the CPRA): you may ask what personal information is held about you, have it deleted or corrected, and opt out of its sale, sharing or use for targeted advertising, without being treated differently for doing so. We do not sell or share personal information and do not use any for targeted advertising.
  • Canada (PIPEDA and provincial laws): you may ask for access to, and correction of, personal information about you, and you may complain to the Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada.
  • Australia (Privacy Act 1988, where it applies): you may ask for access to, and correction of, personal information about you, and you may complain to the Office of the Australian Information Commissioner.

Inside the app, these rights are in your own hands: you can see, export, change and delete everything Cardwell holds, and none of it reaches us. For emails you have sent us, write to connect@oylafinance.com and we will act on your request.

Security

Cardwell's database and settings live in storage that iOS reserves for the app and protects with device encryption once the iPhone has a passcode. The widget file can be read after the iPhone has been unlocked once since it was turned on, which is what lets Lock Screen widgets work. Exported copies and spreadsheets are plain text. App lock adds a Face ID or passcode check when you open the app.

If an update changes how Cardwell handles information, we will revise this page and the privacy details on the App Store before that update is released, and the date at the top will change.
